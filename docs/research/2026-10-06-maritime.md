# Research note: Maritime (YC Fall 2026), 2026-10-06

A research record only. Nothing here changes the design doc, the week 0 checklist or any
decision. It records what was looked up and what was found, for later reference.

## What was asked

The founder asked to look at a "maritime YC company" and how it relates to the Midas
structure. A search of YC's directory found several maritime companies (Maritime Fusion,
REGENT, Albacore, Atomarine), all physical boats, reactors or submarines and not relevant.
The relevant one is **Maritime**, a company named Maritime, not a company in the maritime
industry.

## Facts about Maritime

- Batch: YC Fall 2026. Three people, San Francisco. Founded 2026.
- Founders: Maria Gorskikh (CEO, former visiting lecturer at MIT, researcher at MIT NANDA) and
  Aryaman Shrivastava (CTO, MIT NANDA, research on AI agent coordination at scale).
- YC primary partner: **Pete Koomen**, the author of the Fall 2026 RFS "A Cloud for Small
  Software" that the design doc cites.
- Pitch: "The managed cloud for AI agents." "Give each of your users their own VM, forever."
- Architecture: one Firecracker micro VM per agent on bare metal. Boot in about 0.5 seconds.
  Persistent `/data` directory that survives sleep, restart and redeploy. Idle machines
  hibernate at no cost and wake in about 0.6 seconds on the next message. Optional VNC Linux
  desktop. Runs any Docker image.
- Access: dashboard, CLI, REST API, TypeScript and Python SDKs, templates (openclaw, hermes,
  claude_code and others), `provision({ externalId })` for giving each end customer a machine,
  and "MCP computers" that Claude can drive over MCP.
- Pricing, flat with no per message metering: Free 3 machines (1 awake), Starter 20 dollars a
  month (20 machines, 5 awake), Growth 100 dollars (100, 25 awake), Scale 500 dollars (500, 60
  awake). Sleeping machines do not count toward limits. Marketed as starting at 1 dollar per
  agent per month.
- Customer: developers and companies giving each of their customers an agent. B2B
  infrastructure.

## How it relates to Midas, as discussed

1. **Investor signal.** Koomen is Maritime's partner, so Maritime shows what he funds under
   his own small software framing: per tenant compute for agents, sold to developers. Midas
   answers the same RFS from the consumer side and ends on a phone. This reading is an
   inference, not something Koomen or Maritime has stated.
2. **Opposite answers to the Glitch problem.** The design doc's rule is "an app is a row, not
   a deployment," isolates rather than containers. Maritime gives every tenant a full VM and
   removes idle cost with snapshot sleep instead. Both reach near zero idle cost. Maritime runs
   arbitrary code in any stack; Midas constrains the surface to HTML plus the SDK, which the
   design doc names as the maintenance moat.
3. **Not a competitor.** Maritime sells agent compute to companies. Midas sells private apps
   to a person.
4. **Possible supplier later, not for v1.** A VM per Sidekick at about 1 dollar a month would
   exceed the cost ceiling of 0.35 dollars per app per 30 days, so it does not fit the Sidekick
   path. Shapes where it could fit, all already deferred in the design doc: the maintenance
   agent (nightly tests per app), a generator that runs Claude Code in a sandbox, and jess as a
   persistent per user agent. No stack decision was made; the Cloudflare backend is unchanged.
5. **Draft line for the Nov 2 application**, not adopted: "Maritime gives an agent a computer.
   Midas gives a person their apps: on the phone, private, kept alive."
