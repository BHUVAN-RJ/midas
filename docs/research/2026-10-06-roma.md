# Research note: Roma (YC Fall 2026), 2026-10-06

A research record only. Nothing here changes the design doc or any decision. It records what
was looked up and what was found, for the agent versus app line in `## Bets and Falsifiers,
2026-10-06` of the design doc. The founder saw the launch on LinkedIn on 2026-10-06 and
described it as a notepad where every task written down is picked up by an LLM and worked on.
A first search surfaced Char (YC Summer 2025), the same idea from a different team; that note
is `docs/research/2026-10-06-char.md`. The company the founder meant is **Roma**.

## Facts about Roma

- Batch: YC Fall 2026. Same batch as Maritime. Three founders. Categories on the YC profile:
  Consumer, Productivity, AI Assistant.
- Founders: Jan Senderek (CEO; founded Loom, YC W12, acquired by Dropbox), Tobias Eichenwald
  (co-founded Senic, YC S13, acquired by Gira; programmer and designer, three Red Dot
  Awards), Cody Osborne (creative, Goodby Silverstein, 72andSunny, Dropbox). Two of three are
  repeat YC founders with exits.
- Pitch, verbatim: "The to-do list that does itself." "The original productivity tool
  reinvented so your tasks actually get done." "Work should not vanish into a conversation.
  It should be yours to see, to steer, and to keep."
- How it works, from the landing page: capture by rambling, Roma turns it into a clear task
  title, Roma starts the work on its own in the background across several tasks at once,
  the user reviews the finished work. Tasks are organised into projects and surfaced at the
  right time.
- Execution: an "Operator" with computer access that automates sites and apps. Named
  connectors: Dropbox (find files), Gmail (draft replies with thread context), Google
  Calendar (availability), Linear (create tickets with context), Notion. Connects to ChatGPT
  and Claude as an MCP server so findings can be saved into Roma.
- Example tasks on the site: reply to Maya about billing, send contracts, schedule calls,
  renew insurance, book flights.
- Platforms: Mac (Apple Silicon, version 0.7.2706 at time of writing) and iOS, both
  downloadable from roma.app with no waitlist seen. No pricing published. No user numbers
  public. Founded 2026.

## How it relates to Midas

1. **It is the agent lane, consumer-facing, on the phone.** Roma is the strongest instance
   found so far of the founder's "where there is a need for agents, add agents." The tasks
   are unbounded or one-off (reply, book, renew, send) and the product has no fixed-shape app
   underneath; the to-do list is the whole interface and the agent is the whole product. It
   sits cleanly on the agent side of position 2 in the Bets section.

2. **No conflict with the Sidekick shape.** Roma does not make trackers, lists with their own
   logic, reminders with their own data, votes or shared tools, and it does not produce
   anything persistent a second person can use. It consumes tasks; it does not create apps.
   A tester asking Midas for "a thing that renews my insurance" is asking for Roma, and the
   Bets table already says that if testers mostly ask for that, the app thesis falls flat.
   Roma is therefore a useful reference answer for that failure: it is what the user should
   be sent to.

3. **Same interface bar, same "it is yours" language.** "Work should not vanish into a
   conversation" is the founder's "it should exist, not ask for attention, do its job" in
   other words, applied to agents. Capture by rambling with no form is the design doc's
   "remind me at 3pm tomorrow" baseline. The bar for Midas is not lower than Roma's.

4. **Calibration on team and timing.** Two founders with exits, Fall 2026 batch, Mac and iOS
   shipped and open on launch day. That is the pace a YC consumer team runs at in this
   category, and it is the right comparison for the Nov 2 application's claims about what
   was shipped in the same six weeks.

5. **Watch item.** If Roma adds persistent artifacts (a task that leaves behind a small tool,
   a tracker or a shared list) it crosses into the generator. Nothing on the site suggests
   that; the output of a task is the finished work, not an app.
